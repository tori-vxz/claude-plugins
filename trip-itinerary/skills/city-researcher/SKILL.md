Research vacation activities and notable places to visit in a given city,
including day trips up to 2.5 hours outside the city, filtered to a stated
activity level.

## 1. Confirm the city and the activity level

If either is missing, ask before researching — don't guess a city or
assume a default activity level.

## 2. Apply these activity-level definitions exactly

These are specific categories, not general judgment calls:

- **Low** — sit-around activities: smaller museums or galleries, afternoon
  tea, the opera.
- **Medium** — light walking: shopping, golf, large museums, a riverboat
  cruise, or a hike on largely even ground no longer than 1 mile one-way.
- **High** — lots of walking or physical activity: tennis, exploring
  Pompeii-style ruin sites, or hikes that are hilly and/or longer than 1
  mile one-way.

## 3. Search broadly, but stay on vacation activities

Search only for things to do on vacation — notable and famous places to
visit, attractions, and activities. Do not research or report on
transportation, flights, or how to get between places; that is a different
skill's job.

Cover:

- Notable and famous places to visit in the city itself.
- Day-trip destinations and notable places up to a 2.5-hour commute outside
  the city.

## 4. Filter to the stated activity level

Only report activities that fall within the activity level given — drop
anything that doesn't fit rather than including it as a stretch or a maybe.

## 5. Report each activity together with its link — never a link list at the end

Write the link into the same line or bullet as the activity itself, right
where you report it. Do not collect links into a references or sources
section at the bottom and leave the activities above it bare — that
separates the two pieces of information you were asked to keep together.

Prefer the venue's or attraction's own official website. If no official
site can be found for something, use the URL of the page where you found
that activity instead — you have no write or edit access, so the page
you found it on while searching is the only trace of where that
information came from, and it must be handed back so the link is never
just missing. Never leave an activity with no link of any kind.

## 6. Report in plain English

A short list of options, with in-city places and day-trip places called out
separately. For each, cover what it is, why it fits the stated activity
level, and its link, all together.

Only report what was actually found. If something can't be verified or a
detail is unclear, say so rather than guessing.

## 7. Double-check before replying

Read back over your own list. Confirm every single activity has a link
sitting with it — either its official site or the page you found it on.
If one is missing, go back and attach the page you found it on before
you reply; don't hand back an item with nothing.

## 8. Never write, edit, or save anything

The only output is the reply.
