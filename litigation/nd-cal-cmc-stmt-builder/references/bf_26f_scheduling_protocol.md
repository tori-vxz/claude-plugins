# Bursor & Fisher Rule 26(f) Scheduling Report Protocol — Timing Rules

- **Rule 26(f) Conference:** 21 days prior to the Rule 16 conference, or as directed by the
  initial scheduling order.
- **Initial Disclosures:** presumptively 14 days after the Rule 26(f) conference; alternatively,
  14 days after the Rule 16/CMC conference (often preferable to complete before the CMC to look
  proactive to the judge).
- **Class Certification motion (opening brief):** roughly 8–10 months after the Rule 16
  conference; up to 12 months if an unusually large amount of discovery is expected. Class
  certification experts should be disclosed simultaneously with the opening brief — resist any
  effort to require expert disclosure before the class certification motion is filed.
- **Opposition to Class Certification (and Defendant's rebuttal expert report):** 45–60 days
  after the class certification motion is filed.
- **Reply in Support of Class Certification (and any rebuttal experts Plaintiff relies on):**
  45 days after the opposition is filed. (Note: this reference file's protocol text says 6 weeks
  in the narrative section but 45 days in the summary table — 45 days is the more specific,
  later-stated figure; flag this minor internal inconsistency if it matters to the specific
  case.)
- **Close of Fact Discovery:** preferably not until after a decision on class certification; if
  a judge requires firm dates, propose 90 days after a decision on class certification, or
  alternatively 120–180 days after the reply in support of class certification is filed.
- **Expert (Merits) Disclosures:** 30–60 days after the close of fact discovery.
- **Rebuttal (Merits) Expert Disclosures:** 30 days after disclosure of merits experts.
- **Close of Expert Discovery:** 30 days after disclosure of rebuttal experts.
- **Dispositive motions (MSJ):** 30 days after close of expert discovery, or 30 days after close
  of fact discovery — do not agree to an MSJ deadline before the close of fact discovery.
- **Daubert motions:** 30 days after close of expert discovery; may coincide with the MSJ
  deadline. Resist any defense effort to bring a separate motion to strike experts at the class
  certification stage.
- **Motions in Limine:** preferably not until after a decision on both class certification and
  summary judgment; if a deadline is mandated, set it well after the close of expert discovery
  (60–90 days). Check the judge's standing order for any specific motion-in-limine deadline.
- **Pretrial Conference:** per the judge's standing order — commonly a fixed period before
  trial (e.g., 3 weeks); also referenceable as ~30 days after the motions-in-limine deadline if
  the judge requires one.
- **Trial:** per the judge's standing order — commonly a fixed period after the pretrial
  conference (e.g., 3 weeks), or as the judge otherwise directs.
