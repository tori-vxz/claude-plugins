# N.D. Cal. Standing Order for All Judges — Required CMS Topics

Source: "Standing Order for All Judges of the Northern District of California — Contents of
Joint Case Management Statement," filed pursuant to Civil L.R. 16-9(a). Verify this list is
still current at https://cand.uscourts.gov/rules-forms-fees/forms/ before relying on it — the
Standing Order is occasionally updated (last confirmed update reflected here: Nov. 30, 2023).

This is the baseline structure. Every N.D. Cal. judge requires this same core content; the
statement should not exceed ten pages except in unusually complex cases. Individual judges may
layer on additional requirements (page limits, specific formatting, extra topics, a required
joint proposed order) via their own standing order — always check for one (Step 2 of SKILL.md).

## The 19 topics, in order

1. **Jurisdiction and Service** — Basis for subject matter jurisdiction over plaintiff's claims
   (and any counterclaims), personal jurisdiction/venue issues, whether all parties are served,
   proposed deadline for service of any unserved parties.
2. **Facts** — Brief chronology of the facts; principal factual issues in dispute.
3. **Legal Issues** — Brief statement (no extended argument) of disputed points of law, with
   reference to specific statutes and decisions.
4. **Motions** — All prior and pending motions, current status, anticipated motions.
5. **Amendment of Pleadings** — Extent to which parties/claims/defenses are expected to be
   added or dismissed; proposed deadline to amend pleadings.
6. **Evidence Preservation** — Certification that parties reviewed the ESI Guidelines and met
   and conferred under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(f) re: preservation of relevant evidence.
7. **Disclosures** — Whether initial disclosures under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26 are complete/timely;
   description of what was disclosed.
8. **Discovery** — Discovery taken to date, anticipated scope, proposed limitations/
   modifications, whether a stipulated e-discovery order was considered, proposed Rule 26(f)
   discovery plan, identified discovery disputes.
9. **Class Actions** — If applicable: proposal for how/when the class will be certified, and
   confirmation counsel reviewed the Procedural Guidance for Class Action Settlements.
   (See also Civil L.R. 16-9(b) — additional class-specific content requirements below.)
10. **Related Cases** — Any related cases/proceedings before another judge of this court or
    another court/administrative body.
11. **Relief** — All relief sought (complaint or counterclaim), including damages amounts and
    the basis for calculation; a party from whom damages are sought must state the basis on
    which it contends damages should be calculated if liability is established.
12. **Settlement and ADR** — Settlement prospects, ADR efforts to date, specific ADR plan
    (compliance with ADR L.R. 3-5), key discovery/motions needed to position parties to
    negotiate.
13. **Other References** — Suitability for binding arbitration, special master, or JPML.
14. **Narrowing of Issues** — Issues narrowable by agreement/motion, suggestions to expedite
    presentation of evidence at trial, bifurcation requests. Parties must jointly identify (in
    bold or highlight) 1–3 issues most consequential to the case and how resolution could be
    expedited.
15. **Scheduling** — Proposed dates: expert designation, discovery cutoff, dispositive motion
    hearing, pretrial conference, trial.
16. **Trial** — Jury or bench trial; expected length.
17. **Disclosure of Non-Party Interested Entities or Persons** — Whether each party filed the
    Certification of Interested Entities/Persons under Civil L.R. 3-15; restate the
    certification's substance (financial interest or other interest that could be substantially
    affected by the outcome). In class/collective/representative actions, this includes any
    entity funding prosecution of a claim or counterclaim.
18. **Professional Conduct** — Whether counsel of record reviewed the Guidelines for
    Professional Conduct for the Northern District of California.
19. **Such Other Matters** — Anything else facilitating just, speedy, inexpensive disposition.

## Class action supplement — Civil L.R. 16-9(b)

If the case is (or plaintiff seeks to maintain it as) a class action, topic 9 above must also
include:
- The specific Fed. R. Civ. P. 23 subsections under which the action is maintainable;
- A description of the proposed class(es);
- Facts showing the Rule 23(a) and (b) requirements are met;
- A proposed date for the court to consider class certification.

## Civil L.R. 3-4 compliance (verified against the current rule)

Verified directly against the current N.D. Cal. Civil Local Rules (the version in effect,
confirmed via cand.uscourts.gov — check for a newer PDF at
https://cand.uscourts.gov/rules-forms-fees/local-rules/civil-local-rules before relying on this
if meaningful time has passed). Civil L.R. 3-4 governs the formatting of every paper filed in
this district, including a CMS. Confirmed requirements and how this template satisfies them:

- **Double-spaced, no more than 28 lines per page** (Civil L.R. 3-4(c)(2)) — satisfied by the
  exact-24pt line spacing + margin math described above; the rule's own operational definition
  of "double-spaced" for a 12-point font is whatever produces ≤28 lines on an 8.5x11 page,
  which this template hits by construction. Exception: identification of counsel, title of the
  case, footnotes, and quotations may be single-spaced — matches the "exempt" front-matter and
  caption treatment above.
- **12-point standard proportionally spaced font** (Times New Roman or Century Schoolbook)
  (Civil L.R. 3-4(c)(2)(A)-(B)) — satisfied (12pt Times New Roman throughout).
- **Numbered lines** — Civil L.R. 3-4(c)(1) technically only mandates this for *manually*
  filed paper copies, not e-filed documents; keep it anyway since it's this firm's universal
  convention and matches the real reference filing exactly.
- **Case number followed by the assigned judge's initials** (Civil L.R. 3-4(a)(3)(C)) — e.g.,
  "Case No. 4:26-cv-05924-AMO", not just "Case No. 4:26-cv-05924". Include the initials suffix
  in both the caption box and the footer once the assigned judge is known. **"AMO" (Judge
  Martínez-Olguín's initials, confirmed from the real *Hokes* case number) is only the example
  used when this skill was built — it is not a default.** Every draft must use the actual
  stamped case number from that case's complaint and the actual initials of whichever judge the
  user names for that case; see the case-number/judge-initials rules in `SKILL.md` Step 1.
- **Footer (not header) must state the title of the paper and, once assigned, the case
  number** (Civil L.R. 3-4(c)(3)) — this is a real, specific rule requirement, not just a
  stylistic choice. An earlier version of this template put the running title in the *header*
  instead, which technically doesn't satisfy the rule (and doesn't match the real Hokes
  template either, which also puts this in the footer). Correct placement: two left-aligned
  lines below the horizontal rule —
  1. "Joint Case Management Statement" in 10-point Times New Roman, **small caps** (not
     all-caps — type the source text in mixed/title case so the small-caps formatting is
     visually meaningful; typing it in all-caps first defeats the effect, since small caps only
     changes how letters that would otherwise be lowercase are rendered), **left-aligned** (not
     centered).
  2. "CASE NO. [number]-[judge initials]", also **left-aligned**, on its own line directly
     below — but the **page number shares this same line**, right-aligned via a right tab stop
     at the far right margin (not a separate third line). The footer is exactly two lines total,
     not three: title line, then a case-number-left/page-number-right line.

  **Page numbering starts on the second page, numbered "1"; the caption page has no page
  number.** Put a next-page section break right after the caption so the caption page is its own
  first section; give that first section a footer with the title/case-number lines but **no PAGE
  field** (so the caption page shows no number). In the second section (the body), restart page
  numbering at 1 (`w:pgNumType w:start="1"`) so the first body page reads "1" and subsequent
  pages continue 2, 3, 4 …. Keep the page number's position (right tab at 8640), 10pt size, and
  Times New Roman font identical to the current footer — only the starting value and the
  caption-page suppression change.

  **The entire footer is uniform 10-point Times New Roman.** Every run in it — the title line,
  the "CASE NO. ..." text, and the page number — is 10pt (`w:sz="20"`, half-points) Times New
  Roman. An earlier version set the case-number/page-number line to 11pt while the title was
  10pt; that size mismatch is wrong. Make all footer text the same 10pt size so the two footer
  lines are visually consistent.

  The header is reserved solely for the line-number column — no running title there.
- The rule does **not** prescribe exact margin measurements (unlike California *state* court
  rules, which do — don't confuse the two; CRC 2.107/2.108 govern state court, not this federal
  court). Margins here are a matter of firm convention/reasonable design choice, constrained
  only by the need to hit the ≤28-line/double-spaced requirement.
- Civil L.R. 3-4(a)(5)'s "Class Action" legend requirement applies to complaints,
  counterclaims, and cross-claims specifically — it does not apply to a CMS, so it's correctly
  omitted here.

When in doubt on a formatting question not covered above, check Civil L.R. 3-4 directly (and
any more specific requirement in Civil L.R. 7-2 for motions, which doesn't govern a CMS) before
assuming a stylistic convention is actually a rule requirement, or vice versa.

## Formatting baseline

- **Body-text indent (document-wide) — FIRST LINE ONLY.** Every paragraph of substantive text
  in the document — from the intro paragraph through Topic 19 and the ending signature block's
  surrounding narrative — indents **only its first line** 0.5" from the left margin, with every
  subsequent line of the same paragraph flush to the left margin, and is fully justified. In
  OOXML terms this is a first-line indent (`w:ind w:firstLine="720"`), NOT a whole-paragraph
  left indent (`w:ind w:left="720"`). This is a deliberate change from the earlier full-block
  left-indent style: a reader should see the classic legal-brief look where each paragraph's
  opening line is tabbed in and the rest of the paragraph runs the full measure. This first-line
  indent does NOT apply to:
  - section numbering/headings (e.g., "1. Jurisdiction and Service" — these keep their existing
    tab-based layout, unindented, left-aligned);
  - the caption box and counsel block on the first page;
  - **Section 11 (Relief), in its entirety** — the bold-italic "Plaintiff[s]' Position" label on
    its own unindented line, the intro sentence, and the lettered relief list all keep their
    existing separately-established formatting. Do not convert any part of Section 11 to the
    first-line-indent style; it is a whole-section exception.
  - **the Section 15 schedule table** — the table keeps its own cell formatting. (The plain
    sentence "The Parties propose the following schedule:" immediately before the table is NOT
    part of the table and DOES take the normal first-line indent + justification.)
  - the ending signature and attestation blocks (kept single-spaced and left-aligned per their
    existing exemption from main-text-only rules).
  - **Sections that still split into "Plaintiffs' Position" / "Defendant's Position":** the
    label itself stays bold, italicized, on its own unindented line. The substantive text that
    follows is a separate paragraph carrying the first-line indent + justification — do not keep
    the label and body text in the same paragraph/line.
  - **Sections with no position split** (Topics 1, 7, 8, 10, 13, 14, 15's intro sentence, and
    16–19, per the joint-statement rules): the paragraph itself carries the first-line indent +
    justification directly, with no separate label paragraph needed.
- **Ending signature block formatting (Hokes template format).** For each signing party's
  block: a "Respectfully submitted," line aligned to the same fixed column as the rest of the
  block (see below); then "Dated:" followed by an **auto-updating Word date field**
  (`DATE \@ "MMMM d, yyyy"`, rendering like "August 13, 2026" — NOT a literal `[DATE]`
  placeholder) at the left margin, tabbed to a fixed column where the **firm name in bold**
  begins, on the same line. The next line: "By:" at the left margin, tabbed to the same
  column, where an italicized+underlined "/s/ [Attorney Name]" begins. Every subsequent detail
  line (attorney name with state bar number, mailing address, telephone, facsimile, email) is
  its own paragraph, left-indented to that same fixed column — not tabbed line-by-line, the
  whole paragraph starts there, so everything lines up in a clean aligned column under the firm
  name/signature. The "Respectfully submitted," and "By:" lines both align to that same fixed
  column so the whole block reads as one aligned unit. "Counsel for [Plaintiff(s)/Defendant(s)]"
  (italicized, per the label rule below) stays at the left margin, unindented, after the block.
  Use **3 inches** for this fixed column (not 3.5") — 3.5" leaves
  too little remaining width for several detail lines (state bar numbers, street addresses) to
  fit on one line each; 3" is the fallback the real template's information density actually
  requires. If a future case's specific text is short enough to fit at 3.5", that's fine too,
  but check the actual rendered output rather than assuming — this is exactly the kind of thing
  that looks fine in the abstract and wraps badly in practice.
- **Italicized "Counsel for Plaintiff[s]" / "Counsel for Defendant[s]" label — applies at BOTH
  the front-matter counsel block (top of document) and the ending signature block (bottom of
  document), for every signature block regardless of party side.** After the last line of a
  given party's counsel block, skip one line, then add this single italicized label in place
  of the previous "Attorneys for [party names]" line:
  - Singular/plural driven by that party's actual count from the complaint's caption (the same
    plaintiff-count and defendant-count rules used throughout this document) — "Counsel for
    Plaintiff" vs. "Counsel for Plaintiffs"; "Counsel for Defendant" vs. "Counsel for
    Defendants." This is independent per side — e.g., "Counsel for Plaintiffs" (multiple
    plaintiffs) paired with "Counsel for Defendant" (one defendant) is normal and expected.
  - **Only attorneys/firms actually listed as counsel of record in the complaint itself** get
    the "Counsel for Plaintiff[s]" label — pull this list directly from the complaint's first
    page and signature page (the same source used for the counsel-block information rule
    above). Do not label anyone "Counsel for Plaintiff[s]" who doesn't actually appear as
    plaintiffs' counsel in the complaint.
  - The defense side is always a bracket placeholder in this skill's plaintiff-side working
    drafts — the italicized "Counsel for Defendant[s]" label follows the bracketed defense
    counsel placeholder block the same way, at both the front-matter and ending locations.
- Caption in standard N.D. Cal. format (court name, division, parties, case number, judge,
  document title).
- **Caption box indentation:** the words "Plaintiff[s]," and "Defendant[s]." in the left column
  of the caption box (i.e., the party-designation lines that follow the party name(s) and
  precede/follow the "v.") must each be indented 2 inches from the left margin of that column —
  apply this globally, not just to the Selby example.
- Title: "JOINT CASE MANAGEMENT STATEMENT" (per Civil L.R. 16-9(a)) — even when drafted
  plaintiff-side only for later completion by defense counsel, since it will ultimately be
  filed jointly.
- Numbered sections matching the 19 topics above (renumber/relabel to match any judge-specific
  variant if one exists).
- Signature block for all appearing counsel.
- Some judges require an attached Proposed Case Management Order — check the judge's standing
  order for this.
- **Front-matter counsel block = borderless two-column, one-row table (at the very top of the
  caption page).** Lay the appearing counsel out in a table with two columns and one row:
  plaintiffs' counsel block in the **left** cell, defense counsel's block in the **right** cell.
  Set **all** borders to none/nil — top, bottom, left, right, insideH, insideV — so the table is
  invisible in the rendered/printed document (`w:tblBorders` with each edge `w:val="none"`; in
  the `docx` npm library, each side `style: none`). This matches the uploaded exemplar's
  caption-page counsel table exactly (a 2×1 grid, all borders none). The firm name (first line)
  in each cell is **bold**; the remaining lines (attorneys, bar numbers, address, phone/fax/
  email, and the italicized "Counsel for …" label) are normal weight. If defense counsel is not
  yet known, still build the two-cell table with a bracketed placeholder in the right cell.
- **Caption box.** Two-column table, centered on the page, sized to fit within the actual
  margins (not a fixed width copied from a different margin setup — recompute as
  page width − left margin − right margin, split evenly between the two columns). Text within
  the caption box is single-spaced (`w:line="240" w:lineRule="auto"`) with a space after each
  paragraph (~10pt) — not double-spaced, and not reliant on manual blank filler paragraphs for
  gaps. Borders: the **left** box gets bottom + right edges only (top and left explicitly set
  to `none`, not just omitted); the **right** box gets no border on any edge. This matches the
  real caption table's `tcBorders` exactly (confirmed by inspecting the firm's actual `.docx`).
- **Footer horizontal rule.** A simple top border (`w:pBdr`/`w:top`) on the footer paragraph —
  this is how the real template does it (confirmed from the actual file), not a drawing. Easy
  to set directly via docx-js's `border` property on the footer's `Paragraph`.
- **Page-line numbering and divider lines.** Replicate the exact mechanism confirmed by
  inspecting the firm's real `.docx` file (*2026_07_07_FINAL_Zeus_CMC_Statement.docx*) — this
  is NOT Word's dynamic per-line auto-numbering (`w:lnNumType`), and NOT a single frame with
  line breaks (both were tried first and are wrong/broken — see below). There are two
  independent pieces, both injected into the header so they repeat on every page:

  1. **28 separate paragraphs**, one per number 1–28, each with `w:framePr` (fixed horizontal
     position via `w:hAnchor="page"` + explicit `w:x`, and `w:yAlign="top"` with **no** explicit
     `w:y` — critical, this is what lets them stack in normal paragraph order instead of using
     absolute Y coordinates). First paragraph uses `w:spacing w:line="240" w:lineRule="auto"`;
     paragraphs 2–28 use `w:spacing w:line="480" w:lineRule="exact"` (24pt exact, matching the
     main text's line height). Right-justified, 12pt Times New Roman, no bold, **no paragraph
     border on these paragraphs** — an earlier attempt added a right border directly on each
     number paragraph to approximate a divider line, and that combination (bordered floating
     frame with `wrap="around"`) is what caused the rendering explosion described below. Once
     the border was removed from the frames and replaced with independent line drawings (next
     item), the page count and layout rendered correctly.
  2. **Three page-anchored vertical line drawings** (modern DrawingML wordprocessingShape, no
     legacy VML fallback needed): two closely-spaced lines flanking the number column (the
     real template's double-rule divider) and one line positioned in the right margin outside
     the body text (the real template's right-margin rule). These are a different, more
     standard mechanism than `w:framePr` and rendered correctly in testing.

  Since docx-js's typed `frame` API only supports absolute x/y positioning (not the
  x-plus-`yAlign` combination) and has no API for raw DrawingML anchors, generate the base
  document with docx-js first, then post-process: unpack the `.docx`, inject the raw XML for
  both the 28 paragraphs and the 3 line drawings directly into `word/header1.xml` (before
  `</w:hdr>`), then rezip and run `scripts/office/validate.py` for a structural (XSD) check.
  A working implementation is saved at `references/inject_line_numbers.py` in this skill —
  use it directly (it takes the header1.xml path and handles both pieces) rather than
  reconstructing the XML from scratch each time.

  **Verification history worth knowing:** LibreOffice-headless (the only rendering tool
  available here) exploded the page count (44 pages instead of ~9) and misplaced numbers
  horizontally on the *first two* attempts — one using a single frame with line breaks, one
  using 28 framed paragraphs *with a border on each*. Removing the per-paragraph border and
  moving the divider lines to separate DrawingML shapes resolved it, and the current version
  in `inject_line_numbers.py` renders correctly (verified visually via LibreOffice + the
  user's own check in real Word). If a future change to this mechanism causes similar
  breakage, suspect borders or other properties combined with `wrap="around"` on the framed
  paragraphs first, and always render and visually check before presenting to the user — don't
  assume a plausible-looking XML change is safe.
- **Exact line spacing and font for main text.** All main text — the intro paragraph, AI
  certification notice, numbered section headings, and every Plaintiffs'/Defendant's Position
  paragraph — must use 12-point Times New Roman with *exactly* 24-point line spacing (Word
  "Exactly" line spacing rule, not "Multiple" or "At least"; in OOXML terms, `w:line="480"` with
  `w:lineRule="exact"`). Page top/bottom margins should be set so a full page of this spacing
  yields the 28-line cap described above (e.g., ~0.83" top and bottom margins on a standard
  11" page). The caption box and the counsel-identification blocks at the very beginning and
  end of the statement (the firm header before the caption, and the signature blocks after the
  numbered sections) are exempt from this exact-spacing rule — keep those single-spaced as in
  the reference filing.
- **No extra space between paragraphs — continuous exact 24pt.** Set BOTH before- and
  after-paragraph spacing to zero (`w:spacing w:before="0" w:after="0"`) on every substantive
  paragraph, **including the numbered section headings** (no blank line before a heading), and
  do NOT insert empty "spacer" paragraphs anywhere in the numbered-section body. The uniform
  exact-24pt line spacing alone supplies all vertical separation, so the entire body renders as
  continuous double-spacing at exactly 24pt with no irregular gaps — this is what cleanly
  satisfies N.D. Cal.'s double-spaced / ≤28-lines-per-page requirement and is the look the firm
  wants. The single-spaced exempt blocks (caption box, counsel block, signature and attestation
  blocks) likewise carry no extra after-paragraph spacing and no manual blank filler paragraphs
  beyond the single blank line genuinely needed to separate one distinct block from the next.
  Section 11's lettered relief list keeps its own established list spacing (it is exempt from
  this rule along with the rest of Section 11).
- **`contextualSpacing` on every paragraph ("Don't add space between paragraphs of the same
  style").** In addition to zeroing before/after spacing, set `w:contextualSpacing` (the `docx`
  npm library's `contextualSpacing: true`) on **every** paragraph in the document — body,
  headings, caption, counsel/signature/attestation blocks, and table lead-ins alike. It's the
  belt to the zeroed-spacing suspenders: it tells Word to suppress any style-defined gap between
  consecutive paragraphs of the same style, so nothing sneaks a gap in even if a paragraph's
  before/after is ever nonzero. Harmless on the single-spaced exempt blocks, so apply it
  uniformly rather than trying to decide per-paragraph.
- **Curly quotation marks and apostrophes — universal.** All visible text uses typographic
  ("smart") quotes: “ ” for double quotes, ‘ ’ for single quotes, and ’ for every apostrophe.
  Straight `"` and `'` should never appear in the rendered document — not in body text, quoted
  advertising/product claims from the complaint, defined terms, bracketed placeholders,
  headings, or signature/counsel blocks. In the `docx` build, put the actual curly Unicode
  characters into each `TextRun`. The reference build script routes every text run through a
  small `sq()` helper that opens a quote (“ / ‘) when it follows whitespace, an opening bracket,
  a dash, or the start of the run and closes it (” / ’) otherwise; the helper is idempotent, so
  running it over already-curly text is harmless. Reuse that approach so no straight quote slips
  through.
